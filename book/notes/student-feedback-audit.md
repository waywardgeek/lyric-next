# Reconstruction feedback integration — Chapters 1–8

This audit reads retained student reports, including separate feedback records,
and maps their useful lessons into construction teaching. It is not a new review
campaign, a new grader gate, or a rerun of any passing candidate. Original reports
and results remain historical. No compiler or grader code changed in this audit.

- **Chapter 1:** `ch01-student-report.md`. Two-pass output, byte/scalar positions,
  pinned policy and removal versus destruction are already taught in Chapter 1.
  The missing public grammar-review input is now an explicit authoring bundle
  completeness check; this was setup feedback, not a language correction.
- **Chapter 2:** `ch02-student-report.md`. Complete suffix boundaries, decoded
  escapes distinct from source, consumed-mark blocking, nearest-delimiter newline
  policy and end-first destruction are already at their construction points.
  Unicode-data/source validity and lifecycle/CLI evidence remain distinct.
- **Chapter 3:** `ch03-checks/student/report.md`. Ordinary versus raw spelling
  and complete rollback were already integrated. Chapter 3 now explicitly teaches
  real offline Node workload validation, scoped setup questions and the exact
  143-case fixture argument; a version check or smaller default is insufficient.
- **Chapter 4:** `ch04-student-report.md`. Continuing-path joins, explicit stacks,
  whole-range typing and weak lexical ownership are already taught. Chapter 0 now
  explains that supplementary diagnostic input must reach the intended phase:
  invented syntax and harness failures are not valid compiler rejections.
- **Chapter 5:** `ch05-student-report.md`. Evaluation temporaries, return-frame
  ordering and explicit SIGPIPE startup policy are already taught. Chapter 0 now
  distinguishes hash provenance from behavioral/lifecycle evidence; authoring
  guidance records narrow edits and preserving mechanical build failures.
- **Chapter 6:** `ch06-student-report.md`. Type/continuation separation, preview
  versus authoritative facts, compound snapshots and loop-condition admission
  are already taught. Chapter 3's shared PEG setup now covers Make stripping
  loader settings and the wrong-default-fixture pitfall; later chapters retain
  their explicit required comparison commands. Exceptional unsafe folding and
  private helper names were also audited during Chapter 8 feedback integration.
- **Chapter 7:** `ch07-student-report.md` and `ch07-student-feedback.md`. Loop
  admission before condition traversal and ordered helper obligations were
  already integrated. Chapter 7 now explicitly preserves iterative traversal
  in the new borrow graph and audits old enum consumers during the type change.
  Successful pointer execution is distinguished from invalidation rejection.
- **Chapter 8:** `ch08-student-report.md`. Capacity/group identity is taught in
  Chapter 7; unsafe folding and name-independent helper review in Chapter 6;
  discard cleanup, validity/occupancy, constructor provenance (with the student's
  counterexample) and bounded allocation evidence in Chapter 8.

Useful feedback was integrated into the original construction points, not left
solely in this audit or appended to student exercises as a repair diary. Optional
student diagnostics have not silently become retained requirements. Host setup,
tool refusals and bookkeeping mistakes remain accurately attributed in reports;
they are not invented language rules. Human acceptance remains as recorded in
the book index.
